The challenge

How might we facilitate
the design process for IDEOers?
Design something that fits the way they already work.

01 — Context

An intern,
a brief, and no brief

My internship at IDEO started with a deliberately open challenge: facilitate the design process for IDEOers. No prescribed solution, no scope. Just an invitation to observe and figure it out.


I started where every IDEO project starts — in the field. Watching how designers actually worked, not how they described working.

02 — Research

Watching
before designing

IDEO invests heavily in user research — but the process of capturing and organising findings was slow, fragmented, and deeply personal. Every designer had their own system.


The ideal moment to log an insight is right after an interview — in the car on the way back, while the detail is still fresh. But without the right tool, that moment passed. Notes scattered. Context was lost.

One designer was a heavy Google Docs user. Another believed only in hand-written notes. Both had the same data — and no way to share it with each other.

The wall was not the method. The wall was the symptom.

03 — Insight

The problem was
the moment after

Three distinct frictions emerged from observation:


Capture. Insights fade fast. The best time to record a finding is immediately after the interview — but no tool was designed for that moment.


Format. Notes, quotes, and photos lived in different places. A camera roll, a notebook, a Google Doc — none of it spoke to each other.


Sharing. Synthesising field research required a large physical wall and everyone in the same room. Remote collaboration was almost impossible.


The direction became clear: build something that mirrors how designers already capture research — just on mobile, and shareable.

04 — Design

From a wall
to a pocket

I designed the user flow to match the three tools IDEO designers already carried into every field session: a camera, a notebook, and a voice recorder. The app gave each one a native counterpart — photo, note, and quote.


I then built the app myself in Xcode — responsible for both design and front-end implementation throughout the 8 weeks.

05 — Product

Three gestures.
One field report.

📝

Note

Capture an insight as a digital post-it — the primary unit of field research, now mobile.

💬

Quote

Log a direct quote from a participant. Distinct from an insight — stays in the researcher's voice.

📷

Photo

Attach a photo directly to a research moment. No more disconnected camera rolls.

Share with one tap

Results are wrapped as a formatted PDF and sent by email — no wall required, no room needed. The whole team gets the same view of the research, wherever they are.


I also added deliberate moments of playfulness to make the app feel alive — small interactions that matched IDEO's culture.

Outcomes

An in-house tool.
A conference talk.

"I have been WAITING for this."

— IDEOer, internal review

Field Report was adopted as an in-house app at IDEO — used by designers on live client projects. It was later presented at IxDC Shanghai 2012, recognised as a practical tool that embedded IDEO's philosophy into daily practice.

What started as an open brief became a shipped product — built, tested, and loved within 8 weeks.
